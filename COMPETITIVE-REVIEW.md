# Competitive product review — 18 October 2026

## Evidence and limits

Official documentation describes advertised capabilities, not independent performance validation. Two public interfaces were exercised in the in-app browser: FanFit's Patagonia example returned eight evidence cards; Rephonic's graph index opened, but its The Daily example ended in a client-side exception in this browser. This is one environment/run, not evidence of a general outage. No paid account, card trial, private customer data or competitor source code was used. Other products were inspected through official pages/docs; account-only workflows and their latency were not benchmarked. FanFit's repository README and Devpost page were reviewed, not its implementation. No award or Qloo ranking is treated as proof of market value.

Visual/UX judgment (reviewer interpretation): FanFit offers a clearer first action; Modash's documented profile context motivates editing one candidate without leaving the overview. Partner Desk's table better supports side-by-side commercial constraints than long narrative cards, but that advantage has not been validated with buyers. Rephonic explicitly recommends desktop/tablet for its graph; our commercial desk must remain usable on a phone. No cross-product response-speed claim is justified by this research.

## User feedback and contrary evidence

- [Influencer-platform price discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/influencermarketing/comments/1nycfdt/why_is_every_influencer_marketing_platform_so/): the original poster questions high quotes. Replies also defend data/outreach value and suggest manual discovery as sufficient in a niche. Several replies are vendors promoting their tools. This is anecdotal, mixed and not evidence that buyers need our product.
- [SparkToro discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/DigitalMarketing/comments/1ruvlby/spent_400_on_sparktoro_but_didnt_work_for_me/): the poster wanted unmet-need research rather than audience consumption data and later promotes another tool. Treat as a scope-mismatch warning, not proof of poor SparkToro data. A later commenter separately raises difficulty evaluating one sample topic before purchase.
- A spreadsheet plus a rate card remains a credible low-cost alternative for small lists. Our hypothesis is that explicit unit/channel checks and shareable uncertainty save enough repeated review work to matter. It is unverified. If five target users can do the same task just as reliably in their sheet, avoid expanding this product into a CRM.

## Measurable difference

For one existing candidate's fee: before, **Edit campaign → locate/scroll to candidate → change fee → locate/scroll to Compare → submit**; after, **Update terms on that row → change fee → Save and compare**. The new path has two button activations and one edit, with no unrelated candidate forms. The prior path also has two button activations but adds locating/scrolling through the full list. Do not claim a measured percentage time saving.

For an own list: before, clearing the default six required six individual removal clicks after opening the editor. Now New campaign → confirm clears all example terms and evidence together. Draft safety is preserved by a replacement notice and explicit Save draft.

Engineer-performed scripted/manual acceptance is not a target-user study. Time-to-task and task-success improvements remain hypotheses. Validate with five brand/agency operators using a supplied rate card containing an episode rate, website-only offer and missing market; compare their usual sheet vs this desk. Record median time, number of incorrect endorsements and whether they return for a second brief. Stop expanding if the sheet is equally reliable and faster.
